# Sorting Room by Room Without Overwhelm

If "downsize the whole house" is the mountain, this chapter is the cable car: you ride up one room at a time, have a good look around, and come back down before your legs give out. The method below is built for real humans with jobs, aching backs, and other things going on.

## The cardinal rules

1. **One room. One session. One timer.** Never "the house," never "this morning." A room is finished when the timer says so, and we celebrate.
2. **Every object has exactly four doors.** Keep, Gift, Donate/Sell, Let Go. No fifth mystery pile that grows until it conquers the garage.
3. **The Keep Pile is allowed to be judged later.** Sorting and deciding are different jobs. Sorting fast, deciding slowly.
4. **45 minutes is a full session.** Stop *while* you still have a little energy, not after. Being a little hungry to continue tomorrow is the secret.
5. **The parent holds veto power.** A "keep" you personally find puzzling is their history to keep. Curiosity > correction.

## The 4/3/2/1 supply kit

Bring this before you open a single door:

- **4** pairs of gloves-ish (good gloves for you, thin latex-free for electronics)
- **3** colors of sticky notes (they'll map to the four doors — you'll never need more)
- **2** full sets of tape-and-marker kits, so cleanup never waits on you
- **1** top, flat staging surface (a folding table beats the floor for everything: backs, boxes, decisions)

Color convention from chapter one: **green = Keep**, **blue = Gift**, **yellow = Donate/Sell**, **red = Let Go**. (A note about recycling and disposal: batteries, medication, and electronics have special rules — the Glossary's disposal entry covers safe basics, and your county solid-waste website has the authoritative local rules.)

## Session plan (45 minutes, timed)

| Minutes | Happening |
| --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Set up table, sticky-note "doors," timer. Say the four doors out loud. |
| 5–30 | Rapid sort: everything touched gets a color note *now* — no debating yet. |
| 30–35 | **Hallway break.** Water, stretch, look out a window. Yes, required. |
| 35–43 | Decide pass: process one color at a time. Green last, always. |
| 43–45 | Haul the "Let Go" **out of the room** to a car trunk or bin. Wins stick when they leave the building. |

## Sort order that protects your heart

- **Start emotionally easy:** not the china cabinet. Start with the bathroom, a linen closet, or the guest room — rooms of function, not memory.
- **Kitchen = medium.** Every drawer has one emotional mine in it. Budget an extra break.
- **Save for late:** workspaces, desks, and — biggest — bedrooms and the family-photo zone. By then your family's shared vocabulary ("that goes in the Story box") exists.
- **Top-down, back-to-front:** closets and cabinets before flat surfaces, because flat surfaces are where landing pads turn into avalanches.

## The overwhelm emergency kit

When a session goes sideways — someone freezes, tears start, or everything suddenly feels like a judgment of your parent's whole life — these are your rain checks. Keep them riding along in your pocket.

- **The 3-object reset.** "Let's just do three things and get a cookie." Massive tasks; tiny handholds.
- **The anonymous sponsor trick.** For hard drawers: "Let's imagine a nice stranger from church is doing this. What would they gently do?" Detaching the decision from "I'm failing" is powerful science-ish kindness.
- **The photo-first rule.**Any item you can't decide in 30 seconds gets photographed and stickered blue **[Gift]** with a note "decide next visit." You just bought yourself a gift of future calm.
- **The one-box-visit floor.** Terrible weeks happen. On them, the win condition is *one box of true Let-Go leaving the house this visit.* Nothing else counts as failure.
- **The name-don't-numb rule.** When your parent touches a keepsake and gets misty: "Tell me about it" first, decide second. Objects want to be *seen* before they can be un-owned. That is also what the [Story Catcher role](01-family-meeting.md) is for.

## Green-pile triage (what to do with all those "keeps")

Stickered greens pile up fast. Sort *those* into three sub-buckets at the end of a session:

- **New place** — Goes to the new home. Label with destination room. ("Bedroom-2, shelf")
- **Family** — A gifted keepsake. Write a small tag: *"To Sara, with love from Mom."* Future-you weeps with gratitude — either present tense or maintain the future tense and says thanks. [Heirloom cards](03-heirloom-cards.md) make this official.
- **[Story box]** — Sentimental, but too big or awkward for the new place. Not a failure — invite the camera: photograph the item, photograph a detail, and record one sentence of story. The photo album *is* a real heirloom.

## The staging zone

Choose one room (garage, formal dining room, spare bedroom) to host everything already decided:

- **Left wall = Donate/Sell:** boxes packed and taped, one labeled "DONATE — [charity]" per pickup date.
- **Right wall = Gift:** gift boxes waiting for the promised person, with tags.
- **Center = Keep truck:** only *solid keep* items packed and clearly labeled per new-home room.
- **A folding table = the Late Decide shelf.** Blue stickers go here, sorted by room, nothing hidden on the floor. Blue items only ever move *down* (lower shelf = closer to a decision), never sideways.

Never stage in hallways, never store donate boxes in keep paths — and never, ever put un-stickied items back in an original room "just for now."

## Room-by-room quick notes

**Bathroom / Linen:** expiry dates rule everything (medications, cosmetics, first aid). Sharp items and meds travel separately — the Glossary's disposal entry will keep you safe.

**Guest room / Office:** papers, files, cables, and a million pens pile up here, and everything is at least a little bit a record. Papers of a *legal* character (deeds, insurance policies, titles) don't get sorted in this room — they go in the "Ask a professional" pile from the [Glossary](glossary.md) to review *with your parent and a professional*, never alone.

**Kitchen:** set a timer by drawer, not by room. Commit to doing one drawer entirely per session — lids get matched, duplicates are counted out loud (all the whisks, somehow, every time), and mismatched food containers go to donate after years of "maybe."

**Living room:** biggest furniture questions live here. Take measurements (Chapter 5) *before* opinions form. Furniture decisions go much easier against real numbers from a floor plan sketch.

**Garage / Shed / Basement:** assign these to their own calendar slots, name the heaviest items out loud to someone stronger *before* the session, and never lift a hold-on-a-second object alone.

**Bedroom & photos:** almost certainly a two-session room from the start. Announce it like that, out loud, at the top, so nobody quietly thinks they failed when the timer dings.

## Tracking what happens

Every session, for 60 seconds while tapes cool, between sips: update the [Room-Sort Worksheet](../worksheets/room-sort-worksheet.md) — done/not-done, one-line notes, and which items went to the [donation calendar](04-donation-pickup.md). This is your manual's scoreboard and your proof of progress when your brain whispers "we'll do this forever."
