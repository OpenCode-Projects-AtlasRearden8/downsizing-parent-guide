# Donation Pickup Planning

The unsorted box has a natural enemy, and it isn't willpower — it's **a scheduled pickup**. A truck coming Tuesday turns "we should really sort the den" into a deadline with a motor. This chapter gets items from the staging zone to charities, with logistics you can actually run from your kitchen table.

> Heads-up: charities and local rules vary by city and change often. Everything here is a planning framework — **confirm hours, accepted items, and pick-with-a-fee policies with the charity directly** before booking.

## Step 1 — Build the shortlist (one evening, 45 minutes)

Before any phone call, make two lists:

1. **What you'll have:** rough counts by category from your [Room-Sort Worksheets](../worksheets/room-sort-worksheet.md) — e.g., "6 boxes of linens, 4 boxes of books, 2 armchairs, 1 loveseat."
2. **Who takes what:** search for local charities and note their categories. Typical splits:
   - **General-resale chains** (Goodwill-style): clothes, books, small household, toys.
   - **Furniture-and-household ministries or reuse centers**: furniture, kitchenware, small appliances in working order.
   - **Book-specific**, tool-specific, craft-specific shops and library friends-of groups.
   - **Building-material reuse** (like Habitat-style ReStores): doors, cabinets, lumber, name-brand appliances.
   - **Specialty collectors**: eye-glasses, hearing-aid recycling, professional clothing, pet gear.

Only local-first realities apply here: what's 15 minutes away beats what's theoretically better. Bookmark each candidate's *accepted-items* page, and start a note per charity.

## Step 2 — Set the pickup calendar backward

Donations that "wait for a free Saturday" wait forever. Do it the other way:

1. **Get the moving day date** (or the estate-sale date) from your [Calendar Keeper](01-family-meeting.md).
2. **Place pickups at the end of each room's sorting sequence** — the room *finishes* into a truck, not into more boxes.
3. **Leave a buffer pickup** two weeks before move-out for the stragglers that always appear.
4. **Sellables first.** Anything going to a sale ([Chapter 6](06-estate-sale-vs-auction.md)) gets pulled out *before* the donate pile shrinks it. Reversible order mistake, annoying week.

Rule of thumb: two pickups a month beats one heroic junk-ton day your parent has to witness. Smaller matching pickups are also easier on the neighbors' eyes and your parent's goodbye-by-installments.

## Step 3 — Make the ask (a script that respects the charity)

Most donation lines are a variant of this, so write your own once and reuse it:

> "Hi! We're helping my mother downsize, and we're in [ZIP]. We have roughly [counts]. Two questions: do you do at-home pickup in our area, and are there items you can't take? .. Great. Can we get on a [month] list? .. Perfect, and can you text or email a confirmation with a window? Thank you — this is a big help to us."

**Write down at booking time:** date, window, what charity name the driver will say, whether pickup is free, whether a receipt is left, and the cancellation phone number. Pickup gaps are the #1 chaos source here; the cancellation number is your friend.

Confirm **the day before** every pickup. The 10-minute call saves an entire lost Tuesday.

## Step 4 — Prep the night before (30 minutes)

- Boxes labeled in marker big enough to read from ten feet: what it is, which room it came from.
- Furniture: wipe down, legs' hardware taped to the underside in a bag, drawers emptied and *loose* wrapped in the liner.
- Everything inside the front door or garage, protected from rain, with a folding path cleared. (Drivers generally won't walk past the threshold for liability reasons — staging inside the door is the magic zone.)
- A printed **one-page manifest** summarizing counts by category, taped near the door: saves ten minutes of "what's all this?" and helps the receipt.
- Your parent's choice on presence: some like to wave; it's better to ask than assume they want to watch.

## Step 5 — Receipts and the two lists

Two things to capture for every pickup:

- **The receipt** (charity name, date, list). If you or your parent itemize taxes, talk with your CPA about what's deductible — valuation rules are genuinely professional territory and out of scope for this free guide. Otherwise, the receipt is simply a nice record that good things went to good places.
- **The "where it went" list** for the family shared channel: keeps future family questions short ("the yellow boxes went to Charity A on the 14th") and gives your Story Catcher something to log.

## Small-scale alternatives when a truck won't fit

- **Buy-nothing / neighborhood groups:** fast, oddly emotional in a good way ("someone is *thrilled* about the teacups tonight").
- **Building-material reuse centers:** for leftover lumber, doors, and cabinets, call ahead — some centers accept homeowner drop-offs, some have list requirements, and some partner with contractors.
- **Schools, scout troops, theaters:** love clean junk and craft supplies; a call to the front office is all it takes.
- **Textile recycling bins:** the truly-not-wearable pile — cut off usable buttons and zippers first if your parent likes a small ceremony.
- **The donate-first rule:** when the garage overflows faster than pickups arrive, request *more frequent smaller* pickups rather than storing overflow in living areas. Overflow is a signal your calendar needs another truck, not that your parent needs more shame.

## The emotional part nobody plans for

Items leaving visibly is a milestone, and milestones can hit hard. Ideas: take a photo of the first truck loaded, watch it pull away together, then have the cookie (see [Overwhelm emergency kit](02-room-by-room.md)). If your parent wants a "last look" ritual, honor it — five minutes of goodbyes by category will save you five hours of regret later.
