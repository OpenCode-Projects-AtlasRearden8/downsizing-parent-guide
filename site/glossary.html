Glossary

Plain-English definitions of the words this manual uses — and the ones professionals will use on you. When in doubt, ask the professional to define their term out loud; the good ones love that. Remember: education only, not legal, financial, tax, or medical advice.

People and documents

  • Estate — All the property and belongings a person owns. "Estate sale" is really just "sell the belongings," not a statement about law.
  • Power of attorney (POA) — A legal document letting one person act for another (finances, health, depending on type). If your parent has one, understanding what it covers is professional-consultation territory.
  • Executor — The person named in a will to carry it out. Estate-sale companies often work with executors; that doesn't make your sale a legal proceeding.
  • Heirloom — Any object kept for family meaning. Value is emotional, and sometimes monetary, and the two are unrelated more often than you'd guess.
  • "Ask a professional" list — Our running family list of topics that need an attorney, CPA, or financial advisor. It's in the Family Meeting chapter scripts. When a conversation turns legal-ish, add to the list and continue the meeting.

Sorting method

  • Four doors — Keep, Gift, Donate/Sell, Let Go. Every object gets exactly one.
  • Staging zone — One room hosting everything already decided: Keep truck, Gift boxes, Donate boxes, Late-Decide shelf.
  • Green pile — The Keep pile during sorting, before it's judged item by item.
  • Late-Decide shelf — Where undecided "blue" items wait with a photo and a future revisit date. Items only move down, toward decision, never sideways.
  • Story-grade — A keepsake whose meaning survives as a photograph plus a recorded story (see Heirloom cards).
  • Furniture-grade — A keepsake whose value requires the physical object to continue.
  • One-box-visit floor — On hard days, the minimum win: one box of true let-go leaves the house. Nothing else is graded.

Sales and money words

  • Commission — The percent of sales a company keeps, either estate-sale or auction flavor.
  • Consignment — Handing items to a shop/auctioneer to sell on your behalf, for a percentage.
  • Reserve price — At auction, the minimum acceptable winning bid; below it, the lot doesn't sell.
  • Hammer price — The winning bid at an auction.
  • Buyer's premium — The percent a buyer pays on top of the hammer price.
  • Pass — A lot that didn't sell.
  • Realized price — What an auction lot actually sold for, as opposed to its pre-sale estimate.
  • Appraisal — A professional's opinion of an item's value. Formal appraisals (insurance-quality, IRS-quality) are professional work.
  • Provenance — An item's documented history (who owned it, where it came from). Provenance can multiply auction prices, so keep those family stories.

Logistics

  • Manifest — A one-page count of what's in the day's pickup boxes, taped by the door.
  • Diagonal depth — The corner-to-corner number that decides whether a sofa can actually turn a hallway.
  • Freight elevator reservation — Many senior communities and buildings require booking the service elevator for moving; ask the community office early.
  • Certificate of insurance (COI) — Proof a mover/company carries insurance; some buildings require the mover's COI before moving day.
  • Textile recycling — Municipal bins or programs that accept fabric too worn for donation; check your county's solid-waste page for local rules.

Safety and disposal (bring your county website into this too)

  • Medication take-back — Pharmacies and many police stations accept old medications; flushing or trashing them is usually wrong. Ask your pharmacist — it's their job to know, and they're typically happy to help.
  • Household hazardous waste — Paint, batteries, cleaners, propane, motor oil: your county's drop-off site. Never let these walk into the donation truck; a single leaking bottle can cost a charity its whole inventory.
  • Electronics (e-waste) — Best Buy-style retail drop-offs and municipal e-waste days; wipe drives before any device leaves the house, and ask your parent if any device holds photos you haven't saved (always check this before wiping!).
